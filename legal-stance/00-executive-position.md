# Executive Position

## What Clubroom is

Clubroom is a football-only technology platform that helps independent coaches and clubs publish real-world football services and lets parents, guardians, and eligible athletes discover, book, coordinate, pay for, attend, and evidence those services.

Use this concise public description:

> Clubroom helps independent football coaches and small academies run bookings, payments and player development. Parents use Home to book coaching and see the progress their coach shares. The named coach or club supplies each session; Clubroom provides the platform and arranges card payment through Stripe.

Do not describe Clubroom publicly as a "payment facilitator" until Stripe and FCA counsel approve that term. Do not use unqualified "safe", "trusted" or "DBS verified" claims.

The recommended launch position is:

- **Clubroom’s company:** the provider of the app, marketplace, booking orchestration, payment integration, identity and permission system, communications tools, and platform safety operations.
- **Coach or club:** the clearly identified supplier of the football session and the party responsible for delivery, staff, venue, instruction, attendance, participant supervision, and service-specific safety.
- **Parent or guardian:** the adult consumer making the booking for an under-18 participant. Their authority for child-profile, consent and access decisions must be established for the particular purpose; a family link is not automatically parental responsibility or entitlement to every child record.
- **Adult athlete:** may contract as the consumer for their own service.
- **Child athlete:** the beneficiary and data subject, not the contracting purchaser.

This is an intended legal stance, not a label that overrides reality. If Clubroom sets every material delivery term, presents itself as the coaching supplier, employs or directs coaches, controls performance in detail, or assumes delivery, a court or regulator may treat Clubroom as principal despite the terms.

## What Clubroom is not

At launch Clubroom should not present itself as:

- the football coaching provider for third-party sessions;
- a football governing body or safeguarding authority;
- a guarantor that a DBS-checked or verified coach is safe;
- a medical provider, diagnostic service, or emergency service;
- the employer of independent sellers merely because it enforces marketplace safety and quality rules;
- a bank, wallet, escrow provider, or unlicensed payment institution;
- the venue operator, transport provider, or equipment supplier unless it actually contracts to perform that role;
- an open-ended children’s social network.

## What Clubroom controls and accepts responsibility for

Clubroom should explicitly own the quality of:

- account, role, family, club, assignment, and permission controls;
- seller admission and the accuracy/expiry presentation of verification evidence it chooses to display;
- clear supplier identity, total price, material terms, and cancellation rules before checkout;
- payment initiation and reconciliation through its chosen regulated provider;
- correct booking, invoice, refund, transfer, and audit state within Clubroom;
- privacy, security, retention, access logs, and incident response for Clubroom-controlled data;
- content rules, reporting, blocking, complaints, moderation, and safeguarding escalation;
- truthful ranking, reviews, availability, capacity, and promotional claims;
- accessible and non-discriminatory platform service;
- support for failures caused by Clubroom’s platform.

Clubroom must not try to disclaim its own negligence, misleading conduct, statutory duties, or failure to provide its platform service with reasonable care and skill.

For data protection, the child has their own rights. A competent teenager's confidentiality and safeguarding interests can override routine disclosure to a linked adult. Family and court-restriction edge cases require a human escalation route rather than a simple role flag.

## What sellers must control and warrant

The seller agreement should place real operational obligations—not paper disclaimers—on coaches and clubs. Subject to legal review, sellers should warrant and evidence:

- legal identity, tax status, payment-account ownership, and authority to contract;
- accurate qualifications, affiliations, experience, and verification claims;
- appropriate DBS eligibility/check status where lawful and applicable, without overstating what a check proves;
- safeguarding policy, reporting route, designated responsibility, and legally required DBS referrals;
- suitable public liability and professional insurance at broker-approved levels;
- venue authority, risk assessment, emergency arrangements, equipment safety, and attendance records;
- suitable staffing, supervision, first-aid arrangements, and participant ratios;
- compliance with equality and reasonable-adjustment duties;
- lawful handling of child, medical, emergency, photo, and consent information they receive;
- delivery of the booked service and compliance with the published cancellation/refund policy;
- responsibility for their own employment, worker, payroll, VAT, and income-tax position.

## Liability-reduction strategy

Liability is reduced through layered controls:

1. A limited company separates the founder’s ordinary shareholder exposure from company liabilities, while recognising that directors remain responsible for their own duties and wrongdoing.
2. The app names the actual seller and the capacity in which Clubroom acts before purchase.
3. A regulated marketplace payment provider holds and routes funds; Clubroom does not accept customer money into its ordinary bank account.
4. Seller onboarding, verification, insurance, policies, expiry monitoring, suspension, and audits create evidence of reasonable platform care.
5. Default-deny permissions and assignment-based access reduce foreseeable child-data and safeguarding harm.
6. Fair, plain contracts allocate responsibility without attempting unlawful exclusions.
7. Insurance backs the residual risks contracts and controls cannot remove.
8. Narrow launch scope keeps support and regulatory obligations operable for one founder.

## Profitability and usability principle

Every mandatory control must have a named risk, owner, evidence, and simplest usable implementation. Clubroom should not collect information merely because it might be useful, create review queues it cannot staff, promise verification it cannot monitor, or support payment structures it cannot reconcile.

The business is not ready merely when revenue exceeds hosting. It is ready when contribution margin remains positive after all variable payment and operating costs and when the founder can meet safeguarding, complaints, refund, tax-reporting, privacy, and incident duties within defined service levels.
