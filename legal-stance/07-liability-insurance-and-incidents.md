# Liability, insurance and incidents

## Core principle

A limited company and careful terms reduce risk; neither makes Clubroom untouchable. Clubroom should allocate responsibility to the party controlling it, avoid exaggerated promises, maintain evidence, insure material risks and operate the controls it advertises.

Clubroom remains responsible for its own platform service, including reasonable care in software/agency performance, accurate marketplace presentation, permissions, data protection, payment orchestration, security, complaint handling and the moderation/safety controls it chooses to provide.

The Supplier remains responsible for coaching delivery, staffing, qualifications, supervision, venue/risk/emergency arrangements, insurance, tax, quality, cancellations and service remedies.

## Limits on exclusions

Under English law, Clubroom cannot exclude liability for death or personal injury caused by its negligence. Other negligence exclusions in business terms must satisfy reasonableness; consumer terms and notices must be transparent and fair. Statutory consumer rights cannot be removed by broad wording.

Do not rely on clauses saying “use entirely at your own risk”, “Clubroom accepts no liability”, or that every third-party failure is outside Clubroom's responsibility. They can be unfair, misleading and operationally corrosive.

## Contract architecture

### Family/platform terms

Use a clear, proportionate cap for losses caused by Clubroom's platform service, with legally required carve-outs. Explain that the separately identified Supplier delivers coaching and is responsible for it, without obscuring Clubroom's own responsibility.

Consumer exclusions/caps need specialist drafting and must not attempt to exclude:

- death/personal injury caused by negligence;
- fraud/fraudulent misrepresentation;
- rights that cannot legally be excluded;
- any other category counsel says must remain uncapped.

### Supplier agreement

Require the Supplier to warrant and maintain identity, authority, qualifications, DBS/safeguarding status where applicable, insurance, venue/supervision/emergency arrangements, equality compliance, lawful data use, employment/tax status, accurate listings and delivery/refund performance.

Use targeted indemnities for Supplier-controlled third-party claims, taxes, intellectual property, misconduct and service delivery, subject to advice on enforceability and insurance alignment. Indemnity is only valuable if the Supplier is identifiable, solvent and insured.

### Provider agreements

Review liability limits, data roles, subprocessor terms, international transfers, service availability, KYC decisions, reserve/termination powers and allocation of refunds, disputes, fraud and negative balances. Do not promise families or Suppliers more than upstream providers promise Clubroom unless the residual risk is deliberately accepted.

## Statements Clubroom must avoid

- “Clubroom-approved”, “fully vetted” or “completely safe”.
- “Guaranteed improvement”, “injury-free” or medical/fitness conclusions.
- FA, Apple, club or regulator partnership/endorsement without written permission.
- “Money protected in escrow” or “Clubroom holds your money safely” unless legally and operationally true.
- “All messages are monitored” or “24/7 safeguarding support” unless actually delivered.
- Absolute uptime, data-security or fraud-prevention guarantees.

Prefer factual, dated statements that explain scope, for example: “Identity evidence checked on [date]” or “Insurance document recorded with an expiry of [date]; Clubroom has not independently assessed policy adequacy.”

## Insurance programme

Ask a UK broker experienced in technology marketplaces and youth sport to quote and confirm scope in writing:

- technology errors and omissions/professional indemnity;
- cyber/privacy and incident response;
- public/product liability for Clubroom's own activities;
- directors' and officers' liability;
- crime, fraud and social-engineering loss;
- media/content liability if UGC remains;
- safeguarding/abuse defence costs and any exclusions;
- business interruption/key-person protection as proportionate;
- employers' liability when Clubroom employs anyone (legally compulsory subject to exceptions).

Supplier terms should require appropriate public liability and professional/sports-coaching cover, with evidence and expiry. Clubroom must not imply it has validated exclusions or adequacy unless it actually performs that review.

## Incident governance

Maintain one intake and triage system with separate restricted playbooks for:

- child safeguarding/allegation;
- medical/physical emergency;
- personal-data breach;
- account compromise/fraud;
- payment, refund, chargeback or payout loss;
- discriminatory or abusive conduct;
- provider outage;
- legal demand, regulator contact or media crisis.

Every playbook needs an owner, severity levels, evidence-preservation steps, internal/external notification criteria, communications templates, decision log, legal-hold trigger and post-incident review.

Sensitive narratives must not be copied into ordinary tickets, analytics or broad Slack/email channels. Record access, export, reassignment and override.

## Solo-founder resilience

A solo founder is a single point of failure. Before live child and payment data:

- nominate trained external safeguarding cover;
- retain a privacy/cyber incident contact;
- document provider escalation and account recovery;
- secure company credentials with hardware-backed MFA and break-glass recovery;
- ensure someone authorised can suspend bookings, preserve evidence and contact insurers if the founder is unavailable;
- test restore, incident and payment-reconciliation procedures.

## Sign-off

An English commercial/consumer solicitor should draft/review caps, carve-outs, agency wording, Supplier warranties/indemnities, cancellation terms and complaint structure. A specialist broker should confirm that contractual allocations do not fall into policy exclusions.
