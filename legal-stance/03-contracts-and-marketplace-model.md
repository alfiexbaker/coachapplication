# Contracts and Marketplace Model

## Contract map

Clubroom needs a coherent contract system rather than one universal terms page.

### Consumer side

1. **Platform Terms:** parent, guardian, or adult athlete contracts with Clubroom for accounts, discovery, booking orchestration, payment integration, communications, and support.
2. **Session Contract:** the consumer contracts with the named coach or club for each physical football service.
3. **Booking Summary:** durable confirmation showing the seller, participant, service, date/time, venue, total price, cancellation terms, Clubroom role, and support/escalation path.
4. **Privacy Information:** layered notices for adult users and age-appropriate information for children.
5. **Acceptable Use and Community Rules:** content, messaging, reviews, prohibited contact, reporting, moderation, suspension, and appeals.

### Seller and club side

1. **Coach/Seller Marketplace Agreement.**
2. **Club Platform Agreement**, including authority, staff roles, grounds, assignments, and commercial controls.
3. **Payment Provider Connected Account Terms and mandate.**
4. **Data Protection Schedule**, allocating controller/processor responsibilities by data flow rather than using one blanket label.
5. **Safeguarding and Verification Standard.**
6. **Cancellation, Refund, Attendance, No-show, and Payout Policy.**
7. **Content, Review, and Ranking Policy.**

## Checkout disclosure

Before the parent commits, show for every basket line:

- legal/trading identity of the seller;
- Clubroom’s role as platform and disclosed seller agent;
- participant and guardian authority;
- exact service, date, time, duration, venue, and delivery format;
- total mandatory price and allocation of any transparent fees;
- cancellation/refund/no-show terms;
- material eligibility, equipment, accessibility, and supervision facts;
- contact and complaints route;
- whether separate seller contracts are being formed.

Send this in a durable confirmation after purchase. A mixed-seller basket must not make the consumer guess who owes performance or a refund.

## Cancellation stance

English distance-contract rules include an exemption from the usual 14-day cooling-off right for certain leisure services supplied on a specific date or during a specific period. Football sessions may fall within that exemption, but launch wording and application need English consumer-law review.

Even where the statutory cooling-off right does not apply, Clubroom should publish a fair, prominent, line-item cancellation policy covering:

- seller cancellation;
- consumer cancellation at defined intervals;
- material reschedule or venue change;
- unsafe weather or force majeure;
- child illness and reasonable discretion;
- non-attendance;
- partial basket cancellation;
- provider fee treatment;
- refund timing and method;
- waitlist promotion.

Avoid automatic loss of all money where it is disproportionate to the seller’s genuine loss. Preserve statutory remedies for services not performed with reasonable care and skill or not supplied as agreed.

## Reviews, rankings, and verification claims

- Accept reviews only from a verified relationship to the service.
- Detect, investigate, remove, and sanction fake or incentivised reviews.
- Never sell undisclosed ranking.
- Label sponsorship or paid placement at the point it affects ordering.
- Publish the principal ranking factors in understandable language.
- Do not call a coach “safe”, “approved”, “certified”, or “fully verified” unless the precise claim, evidence, scope, issuer, and expiry support that wording.
- Explain that a DBS check is one input and not a guarantee of future conduct or suitability.

## Independent seller stance

Contracts should reflect genuine independence, not manufacture it. Clubroom may impose marketplace safety, consumer, scheduling-integrity, and data rules, but should avoid unnecessary control over how an independent coach teaches.

Factors requiring employment-status review include:

- Clubroom setting mandatory prices or hours;
- requiring personal performance with no genuine substitute;
- detailed supervision of coaching method;
- exclusivity or penalties for off-platform work;
- supplying all equipment and taking delivery risk;
- disciplining coaches as if employees rather than suspending marketplace access;
- representing the coach as Clubroom staff.

Tax and employment status are separate legal tests. Recheck the model whenever Clubroom’s actual control changes.

## Limits and indemnities

Consumer terms must preserve statutory rights and must not exclude Clubroom’s liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud, or other liabilities that cannot lawfully be limited.

Solicitor-drafted B2B terms may include reasonable caps, exclusions of specified indirect business losses, seller warranties, and indemnities for seller-controlled breaches. Those clauses are only one layer. They do not replace onboarding, monitoring, evidence, insurance, or incident response and may fail if unreasonable or inconsistent with actual control.

## Complaints and disputes

Use one front door for the user, then allocate the case internally:

- Clubroom fault: Clubroom owns resolution.
- Seller delivery fault: seller owns remedy, with Clubroom enforcing marketplace rules and supporting evidence.
- Payment-provider fault: Clubroom coordinates the provider case without falsely promising an outcome.
- Safeguarding concern: safety triage overrides ordinary commercial dispute handling.

Terms must not force consumers through an inaccessible or one-sided process. Preserve rights to statutory remedies and courts. Consider appropriate alternative dispute resolution only after checking sector coverage and operational capacity.
