# Safeguarding and operational control

## Position

Clubroom does not become the coach, club safeguarding officer, venue operator, parent or emergency service. It does, however, design the product through which adults find children, receive information, communicate and report concerns. It must therefore own the safety of its platform controls and respond responsibly to risks it knows about.

Safeguarding responsibility cannot be removed with a disclaimer. The safest launch is a narrow, adult-managed product with little public user-generated content and no unmonitored adult-to-child private messaging.

## Family model for launch

- An adult parent/guardian owns the purchasing account.
- A child is a managed participant profile, not the contracting consumer.
- Authenticated accounts are 18+ at launch; under-18 participants are managed Players in Home.
- Any later 13–17 login model requires a separate restricted-account, authorised-adult, child-rights and confidentiality design before release.
- Family membership is not proof of parental responsibility and does not automatically disclose all child data.
- Guardian permissions must be narrow, purpose-specific, revocable and time-stamped.
- Court restrictions, disputed parental responsibility, competent-teen confidentiality and an allegedly unsafe guardian require a human safeguarding escalation path.
- Photograph permission, contractual acceptance and UK GDPR consent are separate decisions and must never be one checkbox.

## Supplier safety standard

Before a Supplier can accept a child booking, require and record:

- legal and trading identity;
- delivery model: independent coach or organisation-owned session;
- relevant qualifications and expiry;
- the applicable FA-accepted DBS evidence/status where the activity requires it;
- safeguarding education and named safeguarding contact;
- public-liability/professional insurance evidence and expiry;
- venue, supervision, collection and emergency arrangements;
- commitment to the Supplier terms, code of conduct and reporting standard.

DBS eligibility depends on the work actually performed, not the job title. Clubroom should describe factual status and date, not call someone “completely vetted”, “safe” or “approved”. Prefer storing a minimal verification outcome and renewal date rather than a raw certificate image.

Verification must expire automatically. An expired or revoked mandatory credential should prevent new relevant bookings and start an auditable suspension/review workflow.

## Safety-information projection

The family should retain the complete safety record. An assigned and eligible delivery coach should receive only actionable information needed for the booked session, such as an allergy response and emergency contact.

Access must be:

- backend-authoritative;
- denied by default;
- based on current identity, role, assignment, purpose and time window;
- non-transitive through club or family membership;
- withdrawn on cancellation, reassignment, expiry or revocation;
- audited for sensitive read, export, override and denial.

Do not expose unrelated diagnosis history, doctor details, insurance numbers, family-private notes, safeguarding narratives or old injuries to a coach merely because a family has booked them.

Push notifications must never include medical, safeguarding or sensitive child detail. Any emergency offline copy needs privacy/security counsel approval and must be encrypted, device-bound, expiring, revocable and audit-reconciled.

## Communications and content

Launch communications should be guardian-centred:

- operational messages may include the guardian and the identified Supplier;
- do not provide anonymous messaging;
- do not permit unmonitored private adult-to-child chat;
- minimise public profiles, posts, comments and media;
- do not launch public child discovery or follow mechanics;
- reports must support blocking, evidence preservation and timely response.

If user-to-user content remains, Clubroom must assess its Online Safety Act role and complete the required child-access/risk assessments, recordkeeping and protections. The low-operations strategy is to remove nonessential UGC, not to promise a moderation department a solo founder cannot run.

## Incident routes

The product and published policy must distinguish:

1. **Immediate danger or medical emergency:** direct the person to 999/emergency services; Clubroom is not an emergency service.
2. **Safeguarding concern:** restricted intake, immediate preservation, triage by a trained named owner, referral/escalation rules and no unsafe automatic family disclosure.
3. **Platform misconduct:** suspend access/content where proportionate, investigate and preserve audit evidence.
4. **Data exposure:** activate the breach plan and 72-hour ICO decision clock where applicable.
5. **Coaching/service complaint:** route to the Supplier while Clubroom coordinates marketplace records, refunds and any platform action.

Do not promise 24/7 human support unless it exists. Publish ordinary support hours and a distinct urgent-safety route explaining when statutory or emergency services should be contacted.

## Pre-launch controls

- Named internal safeguarding lead and trained external cover.
- Written safeguarding, allegations, missing child, collection, medical emergency and whistleblowing procedures.
- Supplier code of conduct and platform suspension/appeal process.
- Tested restricted incident register with need-to-know access.
- Evidence preservation and legal-hold process.
- Mandatory reporting/referral decision tree approved for England.
- Tabletop exercise involving a concern about a coach and a concern about a guardian.
- Clear interface statements showing who supervises each session.
- Insurance confirmation for abuse/safeguarding defence and platform risks.

## Geographic limit

The first pilot should be England-only. Child protection structures and referrals differ across UK nations. Expansion to Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland requires its own legal and safeguarding review rather than a settings toggle.
