# Clubroom Legal, Commercial, and Platform Stance

Status: founder working draft

Jurisdiction baseline: England and Wales, with an England-first launch assumption

Prepared: 23 August 2026
Review cadence: before incorporation, before provider integration, before beta, before public launch, and after any material business-model change

## Important limitation

This pack is a structured strategy and professional briefing aid. It is not legal, tax, accounting, insurance, safeguarding, payment-regulatory, or investment advice. It must be reviewed by appropriately qualified English advisers before Clubroom accepts real bookings, money, children’s data, or public user-generated content.

## Working conclusion

Clubroom should begin as a private company limited by shares and operate as a football-only technology marketplace. Its intended legal position is a disclosed Supplier-side booking agent that facilitates provider-controlled payments for independent coaches and clubs supplying in-person football services to parents, guardians, and adult athletes. It should not hold customer money itself.

Clubroom is still directly responsible for the platform service it supplies: truthful listings and pricing, permission controls, payment orchestration, data protection, moderation, complaints, safeguarding escalation, and the accuracy of claims it chooses to make. Calling Clubroom an “agent” cannot remove responsibilities created by what it actually controls.

The launch model should optimise for a profitable and usable solo-founder operation:

- one operating company, not a group structure or CIC;
- no Clubroom-held customer wallet or unlicensed movement of client money;
- card and wallet payments through a regulated marketplace payment provider;
- several sessions from one coach or club may share one basket;
- mixed-seller baskets remain a gated later feature because they materially increase payment, refund, tax, support, and regulatory complexity;
- one named contracting seller on every session and line item;
- children never become contracting purchasers;
- no sale of medical advice, safeguarding certification, or a guarantee that any participant is risk-free;
- no external payment for digital content or paid in-app group coaching unless Apple’s in-app-purchase rules are satisfied;
- profitability measured at contribution-margin level, including payments, payouts, refunds, support, safeguarding operations, verification, insurance, hosting, and VAT—not merely headline gross margin.
